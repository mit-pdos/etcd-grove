# etcd-io/etcd#20426: Antithesis found "consistent_index isn't equal to snapshot index"

- URL: https://github.com/etcd-io/etcd/issues/20426 (closed 2026-06-18; labels `type/bug`,
  `stage/tracked`, `area/robustness-testing`)
- Affected: `main` (3.6 development), under Antithesis fault injection: node kills, network
  faults, pauses.
- Fix: etcd-io/etcd#21925 "server: remove verifySnapshotIndex assertion" (`f5641854d`, merged
  2026-06-18), an ancestor of the etcd pin `107edf7e4`. It deletes `verifySnapshotIndex(snap, ci)`
  (`ci == snap.Metadata.Index`) from both places it was called: the receiver's `applySnapshot`
  (after `SetBackend(newbe)`) and the sender's `createMergedSnapshotMessage`. The issue was closed
  with that merge. etcd-io/etcd#20434 was declared to be the same issue (`etcd-20434.md`).

What happened. The assertion fired in two directions, and the thread gives two diagnoses:

1. **Sender, `ci > snapi`** (serathius's reproduction with debug logs, 2026-04-12: `ci` 1344,
   snapshot index 1343). etcd0 is killed. The apply thread had already committed entry 1344 to bolt,
   but raft's `HardState.Commit` (1343) had not been persisted. On restart, `ci` = 1344 from the db,
   raft's applied = 1329 (the snapshot), and commit = 1343. etcd0 becomes leader, and `applyAll`
   sends a merged snapshot at `ep.appliedi` = 1343 while `ci` = 1344. The maintainers judged this
   state harmless and removed the assertion ("after a year of this assertion failing and not
   triggering correctness violation").
2. **Receiver, `ci < snapi`** (ahrtr, 2025-08-06: a received snapshot at index 106 whose db holds
   `consistent_index` 100; once 0 against 107): "etcd1 received a bad snapshot". The fault behind
   the bad snapshot was never identified. This is the case `etcd-20434.md` triages.

## Classification

**F (the assertion was removed before the pin); counterfactual F− at the pin as it stands (secondary
A-pre and A-axiom), F+ under the proposal. The spec already documents the maintainers' resolution.**
At the pin, `verifySnapshotIndex` no longer exists. The state that tripped it in case 1 (`ci >
ep.appliedi`) is one the apply loop's invariant admits on purpose. `loopOk` (`InvApplier.lean:
92-98`) is `st.p = max (ciEff γ st.k) a`, and its docstring says that at start the applied index is
the snapshot's, "at most the stored consistent index", with entries at or below `ci` replayed
without writes (`EtcdServer__apply.lean`, `applyEntryNormal`'s `v3 = false` case). The pinned proof
therefore agrees with the fix: `ci = snapi` is not an invariant. Neither assertion site is checked
at the pin, though. The receiver's site is in the snapshot branch of `applySnapshot`, which is
excluded by premise. The sender's site is in `createMergedSnapshotMessage`, which is an axiom.

Source of verification states: `verified.txt`.

## The counterfactual (step 5)

The "buggy version" is the pin with `verifySnapshotIndex` restored at its two sites. `verify.Verify`'s
spec makes the caller prove that the condition holds (`Verify.lean`: "the spec rules the panic out
rather than assuming assertions are off"). So any proof through a call site must show `ci = snapi`.

- **Sender.** `createMergedSnapshotMessage` is an axiom with postcondition `True`, so the restored
  assertion is never proved, and the axiom would silently be false (it panics in the state of
  case 1). **F−** at the pin, through **A-axiom**. Had the function been proved, its caller
  `applyAll` could only supply `loopOk`: `st.p = max(ciEff k, a)`, with nothing forcing `ciEff k =
  a`. The state `ci > a` is reachable in the model. At start, `a` is raft's applied (the
  snapshot's index), `ci` comes from the db, and the first `applyAll` batch may end below `ci`. A
  crash is not even needed; a crash only widens the window, because raft's commit can stay below
  `ci`. The proof attempt would fail at the assertion, which is **F+ under the proposal**. It would
  have told the maintainers that the assertion was false a year earlier.
- **Receiver.** `applySnapshot`'s spec assumes `ap.snapshot = nil` (raft's no-snapshot contract
  `rnApP`, `InvRaftNode.lean:682`), so the assertion line is unreachable in the proof. **F−**
  through **A-pre**: real followers receive snapshots. In case 2 the condition fails because the
  snapshot is bad (`ci` 100 < `snapi` 106). Nothing here constrains the content of a received
  snapshot.

Case 1's underlying state (bolt ahead of the persisted `HardState`) is an **A-model** fact: there is
no crash model. The verified parts already show that the state is harmless to them. Entries at or
below `ci` are replayed with `v3 = false` and no backend writes (`applyEntryNormal.spec`'s `else`
branch), and `loopOk` covers `ci > a`.

## Proposal: `server-apply-snapshot`

Verify both ends of the snapshot path:

- **The sender.** Prove `createMergedSnapshotMessage` (now an axiom). After `KV().Commit()`
  (`commit_save_au`), the db snapshot's stored index `c` satisfies `c = ciEff k ≥ snapi =
  ep.appliedi`. This needs the conjunct `a ≤ ciEff k` of `server-snapshot-ci-latest`
  (`etcd-21636.md`).
- **The receiver.** Drop `rnApP`'s `ap.snapshot' = null`, and prove `applySnapshot`'s snapshot branch
  under the premise that the snapshot's db has stored index `≥ snapi`, the sender's guarantee. Its
  postcondition re-establishes `loopOk` at `a := snapi` with `ci :=` the new db's index. The
  removed assertion is then exactly the claim that cannot be proved, and the weaker
  `verifyConsistentIndexIsLatest` (`ci ≥ snapi`) is the one that can.

Cost: large. It needs `OpenSnapshotBackend`, `lessor.Recover`, mvcc `Restore`
(`mvcc-restore-from-backend`), the alarm, auth and cluster recovery, the old backend's close
(`etcd-20434.md`), and raft's snapshot delivery (`raft-pending-snapshot-ready`). The sender half is
small to medium on its own and is the half that would have caught this assertion.

## Findings

None new. The assertion is gone at the pin. The bad snapshot of case 2 is undiagnosed, and it is
the subject of `etcd-20434.md`.
