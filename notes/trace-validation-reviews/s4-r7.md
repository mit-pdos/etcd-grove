## First tier

### F1. P9's voter bound has no channel into raft

**The claim.**
- P9: "with a per-link FIFO network (Q5 (a)): 'three voters, and each link delivers in order' … no other candidate of `T` exists with three voters" (lines 404-411).
- The gates are: the configuration, no `ProposeConfChange`, no `RestartNode`, no compaction, no P7 calls, and "each `StartNode` takes its node's `simNode` from the allocation and is called with the allocation's peers (P4)" (lines 505-511).
- The allocation is "`simInv` at `σ_init` (from the peers)" (lines 444-447).
- Plan Q6 (4)/(5) list the same.

**The evidence.**
- Nothing in the gates, the allocation law or Q6 bounds `|peers|`.
- The cursor and clauses (a)/(b) handle only the leader's own delayed `MsgVote` (link `(c, i)`). With five voters there is a second case:
  1. `c` wins term `T` with `{c, x, y}`.
  2. `i` has not voted, and learns `lead = c` from `c`'s heartbeat.
  3. A second candidate `d` of `T` then sends its `MsgVote(T)` on link `(d, i)`.
  4. Raft refuses: `r.Vote == None && r.lead != None`, so `canVote` is false.
  5. The specification grants: `votedFor[i] = Nil`.
- This is round 2's N2, by a second candidate. No ordering on link `(d, i)` excludes it.
- With at most four voters the case is impossible:
  - `c`'s winning quorum (3 of 4) without `i` is `c` plus two voters who voted for `c`.
  - A candidate votes for itself, so neither of those two is a candidate, and `d` could only be `i`, which has not voted.
  - The `lead = d ≠ c` case fails by the same counting.
- So the hook at the `MsgVote` receipt (`Step`'s entry, `traceReceiveMessage`) has an obligation raft cannot meet unless the voter count reaches it. By the note's own standard ("Layer B's other premises enter through the API", round 5's A2), this premise has to reach raft through the API.

**Who it affects.**
- The receive hook's spec for `MsgVote`.
- The allocation law.
- Q6 (4)/(5) as put to the user.
- Layer D, which can only supply what the API takes.

**Repair.**
- Add `|peers| ≤ 3` as a premise of the allocation law. `StartNode` is already bound to the allocation's peers.
- List it in Q6 (5) and in the gate paragraph.
- Say in P9 that it is the voter-count half's channel, and that the cursor is the order half's.
- Optionally say that four voters would also do. `Main` has three.

This also closes T6's arithmetic.

## Second tier

### S1. The compaction permit must be in the storage contract's `Bnd`, not on `MemoryStorage.Compact`

**The claim.** P2: "compaction is the client's (`MemoryStorage.Compact`), so with a logger it needs a permit the allocation never hands out" (lines 366-368). The gate list says "no compaction (P2: the permit)" (line 508).

**The evidence.**
- `StartNode` takes any storage interface `si` under `restartStorage` (`interface/.../StartNode.txt`). Its contract is `isStorageP (snapshotValid γ) (storBnd γ K) (nodeCommitLb γ K) st` (`InvNodeRule.lean:604-605`). The compaction justification is `storBnd γ K c := ∃ k, c ≤ k ∗ nodeAppliedLb γ K k` (`:596-598`).
- A custom storage compacts under `storBnd`, not under `MemoryStorage.Compact`'s spec.
- `MemoryStorage` itself compacts two ways:
  - `Compact` (`J.B x`);
  - `ApplySnapshot`, which takes `J.B idx ∗ J.SV sv ∗ J.DL idx` and returns `memCompacted γs idx` (`MemoryStorage__ApplySnapshot.txt`).
- `J.SV` is `snapshotValid γ` (`MemstorageApi.lean:43`). A client can build it for a snapshot it made itself:
  - `CreateSnapshot` registers `snapMade`;
  - `snapshotValid` needs a committed prefix and `snapMade` (`InvNodeRule.lean:565-571`).
- So a permit on `Compact` alone still lets the client reach `ErrCompacted`, and from there `maybeSendSnapshot`.
- Read literally, P2's text is that permit on `Compact` alone.

**Who it affects.**
- The send hooks of `maybeSendSnapshot`.
- Q6 (5)'s wording.

**Repair.**
- State the permit as a logger-gated conjunct of `storBnd`: with a logger, `storBnd γ K c` needs a permit the allocation never hands out, for every `c > 0`.
- That covers every compaction path at once: `Compact`'s `J.B`, `ApplySnapshot`'s `J.B`, and a custom storage's `Bnd`.
- Write P2 accordingly. Note that compacting even inside the bootstrap log must be excluded, since a probe can reach index `≤ |peers|`.

### S2. The cursor mechanism: where the frontier lives, and where clause (a) lives

**The claim.**
- "`simInv` keeps the frontier `≤` the cursor; the run loop's `recvc` protocol carries '`p` above the frontier' to `raft.Step`" (lines 498-500).
- "(a) a candidate `c` hands out its `MsgVote(T)` before any term-`T` append or heartbeat on each link (`simNode`'s record of its sends); (b) node-local: …" (lines 501-503).

**The evidence.**
- **Stability.** "`p` > frontier" is an upper bound on a value that grows.
  - If the frontier is only in `simInv`'s authoritative state, the run loop cannot keep the fact from the hand-off (inside `Node.Step`'s `recvc` send) to `raft.Step`.
  - Raft knows that only node `i`'s run loop moves frontier(`c→i`), but Iris needs ownership to express it.
  - Clause (b) is "node-local" and mentions the frontier, so the frontier has to be visible in `simNode i` too.
- **Clause (a)'s location.** It is used at `i`'s receive hook, which holds only `simNode i` and `simInv` (the note's own lines 342-345). It cannot be in `simNode c`.
  - It has to be a `simInv` clause on the per-link record of handed-out positions, or a persistent per-term record.
  - It is established at `c`'s `Ready` line from `c`'s `simNode` (its campaign).
  - It also needs uniqueness: `c` hands out at most one `MsgVote(T)` to `i`, since `campaign` bumps the term. That is how a received `MsgVote(T)` at a position past the frontier contradicts (b).

**Repair.**
- Split the frontier of each link `(c, i)` between an authoritative copy in `simInv`, which relates it to the cursor (frontier `≤` cursor), and an exclusive fragment in `simNode i`, which pins it.
- At the hand-off, `simInv` gives `p > cursor_old ≥ frontier`, and the fragment keeps it until `raft.Step`.
- Move (a) to `simInv` (or make it persistent), with "at most one `MsgVote(T)` per link".
- State whether `raft.Step` takes the position premise for every traced message or only for `MsgVote`. The self-addressed messages that `Advance` steps (link `(i, i)`) never pass a cursor.

### S3. `QuorumLogInv`: round 6's S2 repair is only partly made

**The claim.** Table row (line 353): the copy over a per-node set, "with a bag clause: every successful append response handed out is in its source's set", used by "`QuorumLogInv` (at a `Commit` line, from the leader's match-index quorum: its members' responses are in their sets)".

**The evidence.**
- `quorumLogInv` quantifies over every server `i` and its `Committed(i)` (`Invariants.lean:66-71`), not over leaders at their `Commit` lines.
- A follower's commit comes from an append request's `commitIndex`. Its quorum is the leader's quorum at some earlier `Commit` line, and nothing in the table records that quorum.
- Round 6's S2 asked for "a σ-level commit record (extend the commit-point clause to name the quorum, or carry the record with each request's commit)". Revision 7 does not have it.
- The bag clause covers responses in the bags, but the `Commit` line uses `σ.s l.matchIndex`, whose responses have already been received. The row needs two more clauses:
  - a matchIndex clause: "`matchIndex[l][j]` is 0, or `(T, matchIndex[l][j]) ∈ S_j`, or at most `j`'s commit";
  - "the sets only grow".
- The commit-point row's `Match` clause is about `E_T`, not the sets.
- Raft's `cq` is still needed at the `Commit` line, for `lc_core`/`cq_agree` to exclude `hist`'s divergence disjunct below `n`. `maybeCommit` gives it (`commitIn`). Say that too.

**Repair.** Add to the table:
- the `matchIndex`-to-set clause;
- the sets' monotonicity;
- a cluster-wide commit record: every server's commit index, and every `commitIndex` in an append request or heartbeat in the bags, is at most some `n` with a recorded `(T, n, Q)`. Here `Q` is a quorum whose members `j` have `(T, m_j ≥ n) ∈ S_j` or `m_j ≤ commit_j`, and raft's `cq γ T n _` holds.

Then `QuorumLogInv` follows at every state, from the record and `hist`.
