# TCP at the `net` level: the extension, its specs, and the examples

Started 2026-11-12 (branch `net-tcp`). The goal (the user's): a semantics of Go's `net` TCP
connections as a GooseLang extension, whose global state is the network's connections with their
sent-but-not-received bytes, kept across a machine's crash; generic Iris specs over network
points-to resources; and minimal programs that use `net.Conn` as real clients do (net/http,
grpc-go), each verified end to end with a distributed adequacy theorem.

**Renamed 2026-11-18** (the user's decision; branch `worktree-agent-a32f38f0bf217c29e`, phase 1 of `notes/dsverify-net-port-plan.md`): it is *the net extension*. `GooseLang/Ext/Tcp/` is `GooseLang/Ext/Net/` (namespace `Perennial.Tcp` -> `Perennial.Net`; `tcpExt`, `TcpGlobal`, `TcpStep`, `tcpEnd`, `wp_tcp_*`, ... -> `netExt`, `NetGlobal`, `NetStep`, `netEnd`, `wp_net_*`, ...), the part `NetTcp/` is `Net/` (project `net`, `lake exe net-replay`, `etc/tcb-collections/net.txt`, `NETTCP_*` -> `NET_*`). The sections below keep the old names (history), but "Where things are".

## The semantics (v3.2, 2026-11-15)

**Only observable state** (the user, 2026-11-14: "make sure the semantics is not designed for proof
convenience. It should be easy to audit, and as a general principle, operational semantics and
specs should avoid unobservable state (e.g., the past operations are unobservable)";
`.claude/skills/spec/PRINCIPLES.md`). The global state is the plan (address → host) and the
connections; a connection is its address, whether accepted, two ends (`TcpEnd`: `closed`, a read
deadline, a write deadline) and two directions (`TcpDir`: the unread bytes, oldest first, and a
status `open | fin | cut`). A node's local state is its host and its listeners. No history.
Decisions D1–D22 are in `Impl.lean`'s header, each with its Go test (`examples/net/semantics/doc.go`,
every row classed measured, documented with a citation, or assumed, and naming its Lean definition
and its `Tests.lean` example).

**Each operation's outcome is a pure relation** on one end's state and one direction
(`ReadOutcome`/`ReadAvail`, `WriteOutcome`, `CloseOutcome`, `CloseWriteOutcome`, `DeadlineOutcome`);
the step looks the connection up, takes an outcome and writes it back, and the specs state the same
relations verbatim, so what an auditor checks against Linux is exactly what proofs get.

**Faults.** A connection may break at any read or write: the direction becomes `cut`, a prefix of
its unread bytes survives, and the read answers as on a broken direction (bytes, EOF or an error).
That covers resets, kernel timeouts, and the death of a peer's process (its kernel's FIN or reset:
`TestKilledWriterFin`, `TestKilledResetTakenByWrite`) or machine. So **EOF guarantees nothing**
beyond "no more bytes will come"; what a reader read is a prefix of what the writer wrote (order, no
invention), never a promise of completeness. Timeouts only with a deadline set, and they lose no
byte. A failed `Dial` may leave a connection the listener accepts, reading EOF (D16:
`TestDialTimeoutAccepted`).

**Errors are Go's values** (D14). Every failure has a kind, `closed` (the caller's own end or
listener is closed: `net.ErrClosed`), `timeout` (a deadline: `os.ErrDeadlineExceeded`; a
`DialTimeout` also `net`'s `errTimeout`, both shapes measured) or `sys` (an `*os.SyscallError` around
a nonzero `syscall.Errno`, with Go's syscall name), wrapped in an `*OpError` with Go's `Op`, `Net`,
`Source`, `Addr`; `OpError` and `Unwrap` are translated, `Timeout()`/`Temporary()` are true for
deadlines, false for `ErrClosed`, arbitrary for `sys` (a kernel `ETIMEDOUT` reports both true:
`TestTimeoutOnBreak`). Unlike dsverify-lab's model, which returns an error type of its own (refutable
by a type assertion), these are Go's own values (Perennial translates `internal/poll`, `os`).

**A `Write` is Go's loop** (D2, D15; v3.1): `poll.FD.Write` is a loop of `write(2)` calls with
waits, so the Go-level `Write` is a loop of extension steps (`net.model.writeLoop`), each one call on
the bytes not yet written, checking the end's state at that step: some bytes appended (all, some, or
none: a wait), or a failure appending nothing (`sys` breaking the direction; `timeout` with a write
deadline set at that step; `closed` once the end is closed). A `Close` or a deadline set between two
steps gives Go's `(n, ErrClosed)` and `(n, ErrDeadlineExceeded)` with `0 < n < len`
(`TestWriteCloseRace`, `TestWriteDeadlineSetDuring`). `Close` of an open end or listener succeeds
(D17).

**Addresses** (D18): `LocalAddr`, `RemoteAddr`, `TCPListener.Addr` are non-nil `*TCPAddr`s fixed at
creation, otherwise arbitrary (in the Go heap, no extension state). `DialTimeout` (D19). Wildcard
`Listen` (`":p"`, `"0.0.0.0:p"`, `"[::]:p"`, D21) listens at the plan address with port `p` at this
node's host, stuck unless the port is unique in the plan.

**Crashes** are node-local (an extension's crash relation sees only the node's local state): the
node's listeners are gone, connections stay; what the peers see of the dead node's connections is
covered by cuts. For the same reason (a step sees only its own node and the network) pending
connections belong to the address, not to a listener, and `Dial` does not check for a listener:
more behaviors than Linux (which refuses or resets), each sound.

**Steps** (D22, v3.2; the user's decision, Perennial's Grove convention): every operation may
stutter (nothing changes, the call stays as it was), which is how a blocked `Read`, `Accept` or
`Write` step is modeled; the relations have no wait of their own (a non-failing `Write` step counts
at least one byte unless none remain). Every state has an outcome besides the stutter (else a
program could stutter forever where Go goes on): an argument the extension cannot use panics
(GooseLang's stuck builtin `Panic`): `Listen` or `Dial` outside the plan (a violation of D13), an
id naming nothing, another argument shape, an unknown operation. `Impl.lean`'s header has the table
of every operation in every state; `Tests.lean`'s `isTcpStep_total` checks that no state lacks an
outcome. The specs handle the stutter once, by Löb induction (`wp_tcp_op`, as Perennial's
`wp_GroveOp`).

**Handles**: ids inside `net`'s unexported structs; id 0 (a zero-value `TCPConn`/`TCPListener`)
names nothing, and the models check for it first, returning Go's bare `syscall.EINVAL`
(`model.einval`, `TestZeroValues`).

**The deployment** (D13) every theorem assumes: a closed world (nothing outside the system uses the
plan's addresses; prose only), plan addresses that are canonical IPv4 `a.b.c.d:p` with a port
outside the ephemeral range (D20: `PlanWellFormed`, decidable, a premise of every top-level theorem,
checked by `decide`), and the target configuration (Linux 7.0 defaults, Go 1.27.3).

### History of the design

- **v1 (2026-11-12/13)**: each direction was "every byte ever sent" plus a read count, an end had a
  `broken` flag (a failure with no deadline) and a broken end sent no FIN, so EOF meant the writer
  had finished cleanly. I chose the history for proof convenience (a monotone list gave lower
  bounds directly) and did not raise it, though the user had asked for the unread bytes: the
  departure the principle above now rules out. A comparison with dsverify-lab's network model found
  two unsound points: EOF without a FIN after a reset was excluded, and a node crash was a machine
  crash (no FIN) while the examples ran on one machine, where a crash is a process kill, which FINs:
  halfclose's guarantee had no real deployment.
- **v2 (2026-11-14)**: the state above; the measured tests (`examples/net/semantics/`) refuted its
  first draft (a break returned only an error; a killed process's FIN gives a plain EOF), fixed by
  letting a break answer as a read of the broken direction.
- **v2.1 (2026-11-14)**: the soundness review (`notes/axiom-review/tcp-semantics.md`) refuted v2
  with a proved counterexample (an empty `Write` could only succeed), and found id 0 naming a
  connection end and the deployment unstated; all fixed.
- **v3 (2026-11-14)**: what we took from dsverify-lab's network model (`notes/net-tcp-v3-plan.md`,
  `notes/net-tcp-dsverify-survey.md`): Go's error values, the Write/Close race, abandoned dials,
  addresses, `DialTimeout`, the deployment as a decidable premise, wildcard `Listen`, Lean tests and
  evidence classes, namespace tests, the conformance checker.
- **v3.1 (2026-11-14/15)**: the v3 review refuted the one-step `Write` (a deadline set while it
  blocks makes Go's `Write` time out after the peer read some of it): a `Write` is now Go's loop of
  steps, each `write(2)` with its wait one extension step (D2, D15; E11).
- **v3.2 (2026-11-15)**: every operation may stutter (the waiting), invalid uses panic, every state
  has an outcome (D22; E12, the user's decision: "having an obligation to show `∃ e', e ⇝ e'` is a
  sort-of bogus way of encoding that a program doesn't do something invalid"); the zero values
  return `EINVAL` as Go's do. The extension's and `net`'s specs and `wire` adapted (statements
  unchanged but `ReadRes.wait` gone from `Conn.Read.spec`); the seven examples' proofs are being
  redone over it.
- **v3.3 (2026-11-19, branch `net-gaps`, pending the user's review)**: phase 2 of
  `notes/dsverify-net-port-plan.md`, the gaps the dsverify kits hit (`notes/decisions.md`, "The net
  extension v3.3"): the network `"tcp4"` (D23: as `"tcp"`, the network kept in the `TCPConn` and
  `TCPListener` and put in their errors' `Net`); socket options (D24: `SetKeepAlive`,
  `SetKeepAlivePeriod`, `SetNoDelay`, `SetLinger`, `SetReadBuffer`, `SetWriteBuffer`, one stateless
  `"SetSockopt"`, `closed` on a closed end, `EINVAL` for a keepalive period over 32767 s); Go's write
  lock (D25: a `Write`'s first step takes the end's lock, its last releases it, so two `Write`s of
  one end never interleave; v3.1's loop had let them). Measured in `examples/net/semantics/{tcp4,sockopt,writelock}_test.go`
  (`TestTcp4`, `TestSockOpts`, `TestWriteLock`); the conformance checker transcribes the lock (no more
  "split" of overlapping `Write`s) and a probe mode `concwrite` adds a certified sample. `IP.To4`
  translated; `SplitHostPort`, `ParseIP`, `IP.String`, `IP.Equal` stay names only (below,
  "Address helpers"). Review: `notes/axiom-review/tcp-semantics.md`, v3.3.
- **v3.4 (2026-11-20, branch `net-noplan`, the user's request for kvraft's top-level theorem,
  `notes/kvraft-robust-goal.md` on branch `worktree-kvnet-toplevel-statement`)**: **no plan and no
  hosts**. `NetGlobal` is the connections only and `NetLocal` the listeners only (each the string
  given to `Listen`). An address is valid if `addrOk` (D20: canonical `a.b.c.d:p`, IPv4 unicast,
  port nonzero and outside the ephemeral range; moved from `Deploy.lean` into `Impl.lean`); `Listen`
  takes a valid address or a wildcard with a valid canonical port (`listenOk`, D21), `Dial` a valid
  address; anything else panics (D22). `Listen` gives a new listener or `sys`, only `sys` if this
  node (process) already has an open listener at that string; **any node may listen at any valid
  address**. `Accept` takes any connection pending at an address its listener takes (`lisAccepts`:
  its own string, or for a wildcard any address with its port, D7, D21). `Dial`, `Read`, `Write`,
  `Close`, `CloseWrite`, the deadlines, `LisClose` and crashes (the listeners dropped, the
  connections kept) are unchanged. Initial states: `NetLocal.init` (no listener), `NetGlobal.init`
  (no connection); `crash_init`: a crash leaves a machine in `init`.

  *It only adds behaviours.* Every v3.3 step is a v3.4 step: a v3.3 `Listen` resolved to a plan
  address at this host, which is valid (`PlanWellFormed`) or a wildcard with a canonical valid port;
  v3.4 stores the string given (`a`, not the resolved `a0`) and its `Accept` takes every connection
  v3.3's did (`a0` has the wildcard's port, so `lisAccepts a a0`); a plan address is valid, so every
  v3.3 `Dial` is a v3.4 one. The new steps all start from a state and argument where v3.3 panicked
  (a `Listen` at another host's address, or at a valid address not in the plan; a `Dial` of a valid
  address not in the plan; a wildcard whose port was not unique in the plan), and a `Panic` was
  never a step a proof allowed (D22: a proof shows the program never makes the call). So a program
  proved robust under v3.3 is robust under v3.4, and v3.4's theorems need no premise about a plan.

  *Soundness against Linux* (the target configuration, D13). (1) A `Listen` the kernel refuses is a
  failure the model allows: every `Listen` may fail `sys`, which covers `EADDRNOTAVAIL` (the address
  is not one of this machine's, `TestListenNotLocal`) and `EADDRINUSE` (another socket, of this
  process or another, holds it: `TestListenTwice`, `TestListenTakenByOtherProcess`); the only
  outcome the model excludes is a second successful `Listen` at a string this node (process)
  already listens at, which Linux refuses (`EADDRINUSE`: Go sets `SO_REUSEADDR`, not
  `SO_REUSEPORT`); two nodes on one machine may both succeed in the model, which only adds
  behaviours. (2) A connection the kernel delivers is one the
  model's `Accept` may return: a connection dialed to the valid address `a` reaches the machine
  that owns `a`'s IP, and there a listener bound to `a` itself or to a wildcard with `a`'s port
  (canonical spellings do not alias, D20, `TestAddressAliases`); that listener, at `a` or at the
  wildcard, takes `a` in the model (`lisAccepts`), and the model lets any machine's listener that
  takes `a` accept it, the owner's among them. The model allows more: a listener at `a` on a
  machine that does not own `a` cannot exist on Linux (its `Listen` failed), and a wildcard takes
  only local addresses on Linux, any in the model. (3) Pending connections belong to the address
  and survive a crash (D7, unchanged): a connection dialed to a crashed incarnation may be accepted
  by the next (more than Linux, which resets it; the dialer's view of a reset is a cut, which the
  model allows too). (4) The closed world, restated without the plan, in terms of nodes and
  reachability (the v3.4 review's F1-F3, `notes/axiom-review/tcp-semantics.md`): no process other
  than the deployment's nodes, on any machine (the deployment's own included), accepts a
  connection a node dials, or dials a connection a node's listener can accept (a squatter
  listening at a server's address would accept its clients, `examples/axiomcx/tcp-squat`; an
  outside dialer would make connections no node's `Dial` made).

  Which machine listens where is the proofs' business now: the start resources give every valid
  address's pending set (finitely many valid addresses, `Deploy.lean`'s `addrList`; `netStart`),
  and a proof keeps exclusivity in its own ghost state. Updated: the specs (`net.Listen.spec` takes
  `listenOk`, `net.Dial.spec`/`DialTimeout.spec` and `Listener.Accept.spec` take `addrOk`, no
  `netPlan`), the kits' `DistDeploy` (distinct valid addresses, no connection, no listener) and
  bridge, the seven examples (their plan and host premises dropped), the Lean replay (witness
  version 2) and the Python checker (`examples/net/conform/check/README.md`), the Go examples
  (`TestListenNotLocal`, the D6/D7/D13/D20/D21 rows of `examples/net/semantics/doc.go`). Review:
  `notes/axiom-review/tcp-semantics.md`, v3.4.

## The resources and specs

`Ghost.lean`: `tcpEnd c s st` (the end's state), `tcpDir c s d` (the direction from end `s`,
exclusive), `tcpConnAt c a`, `tcpPend a S` (the connections dialed to `a` and not yet accepted),
`tcpPlan a h`, `tcpLis l a o` and `tcpLisAt l a` (persistent: the listener exists). The extension's
WPs and the Go-level `net` specs are logically atomic over them, with the outcome relations as their
post; a waiting operation (`Read`, `Accept`) opens its update only at the step that decides, so ends
and listeners can live in invariants shared by threads (duplex's reader and writer, shutdown's
blocked `Accept` and `Read`).

**Wildcard listeners (D21; 2026-11-23, branch `net-wildcard-listen`).** No change to the semantics:
`Impl.lean` already lets `Listen` take a wildcard (`listenOk`) and its `Accept` take a connection
dialed to any valid address with its port (`lisAccepts`). The specs now cover it, one canonical spec
per function: `net.Listen.spec` takes `listenOk a` and the right to listen at `a`, `lisToks a` (the
listening tokens of every valid address `a` takes: `a`'s own for a valid address,
`lisToks_addrOk`, every valid address with its port for a wildcard), and gives `netLisOn l a`
(`Ghost.lean`: the listener exists, at a valid address or a wildcard; `netLisAt` is it at a valid
address). `net.Listener.Accept.spec` takes two updates: one for a failure, over the listener's state
only, and one, for every valid address `a` the listener takes, over the listener's state and `a`'s
pending set; its post names the address the connection was dialed to (`isConn c cn true a`,
`Qok a cn`). `Listener.Close.spec` takes `netLisOn`. The former shapes, at a valid address, are
instances, proved from the canonical specs (`Net/Proof/net/AtAddr.lean`: `Listen.spec_addrOk`,
`Listener.Accept.spec_addrOk`), which the existing callers use unchanged. The kits' vocabulary
(`Proto.lean`): `lisUnset la` (every address `la` takes unset, from the start resources beside a list
of single addresses: `addrs_lis_start`, the listeners pairwise disjoint and disjoint from the list),
`lis_set_proto` (one pair of protocols for all of them: `lisProto la P Q`), `lisProto_addr` (each
address's `addrProto`, so a dialer dials any of them with `dial_au`), `listen_lis_au`,
`accept_lis_au` and `accept_lis_shared_au`. `addrProto`'s listening token is in an invariant of its
own or in that of a `lisProto` (`lisTokInv`), so `listen_au` works at an address a wildcard covers.
What the semantics does not give: a wildcard takes every address with its port in the model, on every
machine (Linux: only its machine's, `TestWildcardListen`), so two machines listening on the same port
through wildcards cannot be told apart (their `lisUnset`s overlap); dsverify-lab's kits use a
distinct port per node (`notes/dsverify-net-port-plan.md`, phase 0), so `lisProto` per port fits
them. Telling machines apart would need the machine's addresses in the semantics (dsverify-lab's
`nodeIp`), a trusted change.

**What proofs add on top, as ghost state** (`wire/Stream.lean`, proved, not trusted): per direction,
the writer's written bytes and the reader's read bytes, tied to the unread bytes by `dirInv`
(`DirOk`: read ++ unread = written until a cut, a prefix after); `read_au` gives `sentLb` (what was
read was written). The examples' protocols are built from it. `wire.ReadFull`'s spec takes a reader
predicate of the bytes it read (the buffer's contents) and a per-`Read` accessor.

## The examples

All seven were proved end to end over v3.1 (`lake build`, 2026-11-15; over v3.2 they are being
redone), each `<ex>_crash_safety` over
`goose_dist_crash_adequacy` with no `sorry` and no axiom of this repository, under the plan premise
`PlanWellFormed pl` (D20); `#print axioms` lists Lean's three, the translation's axiomatized Go types
(since v3 also `os.Process`/`os.processHandle` and the value `os.blockSize`, from the `os` translation
`net` now imports), and for preface Perennial's `time.Now.wp_proof`. Each example handles abandoned
dials (the dialer deposits the connection's usual protocol, which a shut, empty direction satisfies)
and every error kind (a non-nil `*OpError`, `net.resErr_netErr`); a writer with a protocol of its own
keeps it per step through `Conn.Write.spec`'s accessor (echo, acceptloop and preface's servers now
record their written bytes in a ghost variable in place of v3's one-time token).

**Each node is its own process** (since 2026-11-14): a command of its example,
`examples/net/<ex>/cmd/<node>/main.go`, whose `main` calls the example's body (`Serve`, `Call`,
`SendJunk`, `Run`) and may exit (a process's exit or kill is a break for its peers, D10/D11). The
top-level theorem is the distributed adequacy theorem of exactly those programs: node `i` boots its
command's package initializer and then its `main` (`boot_<node>`, with the command's own package
table, `<cmd>.getIsPkgInit_wf`), and `cmd/<node>/Main.lean`'s `wp_main` proves the `main` from the
body's theorem (the example's `isPkgInit` from the command's, `iPkgInit`). The process tests
(`process_test.go`) build the commands and run them as separate processes over real TCP: each
client node again and again against the server, each exiting 0, and the server killed (SIGKILL) and
restarted under running clients, none of which may panic. (All seven checked by `lake build`, 2026-11-14.) Until then, each node's program was the
body followed by `wire.Forever()` (a process that never exits), because v1's semantics had no FIN
on exit; v2's breaks cover it, so `Forever` is gone.

| Example | Theorem | Nodes | Shape of the proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| echo | `echo_crash_safety` | 3 | the dialer's deposit; one invariant over a ghost map of names per connection (both directions' `dirInv` bodies, the server's written bytes a prefix of a lower bound of the client's); each direction's `inv` derived (`inv_alter`) |
| framed | `framed_crash_safety` | 3 | as echo, many writes: a ghost copy of the server's bytes for the serving thread |
| acceptloop | `acceptloop_crash_safety` | 2 | echo's protocol per connection; goroutines per connection and per call, the client's joined by a `WaitGroup` (`Join.lean`'s `wp_Add_many`: Perennial's join idiom has `Add(1)` only) |
| duplex | `duplex_crash_safety` | 2 | the client's end shared by its writer goroutine and its `ReadFull` reader through an invariant |
| halfclose | `halfclose_crash_safety` | 2 | the reply is a prefix of the request (the check was weakened: EOF proves nothing) |
| preface | `preface_crash_safety` | 3 | deadlines set and cleared (`DeadlineOutcome`, Go's `Time.IsZero`) |
| shutdown | `shutdown_crash_safety` | 1 | a listener and an end shared with blocked goroutines; every operation after `Close` fails |

## Conformance checking (E10)

Real runs are checked against the semantics, as dsverify-lab's `harness/conform` does, with its
verdicts certified in Lean:
- **Tracer** (`examples/net/conform/`): a `go build -overlay` of Go's own `net` sources records every
  modeled call (begin and end, monotonic time, the arguments, the results, the error's facts) and
  the unmodeled ones, one JSON line per `write(2)`; `NETTCP_CONFORM=1` makes `proctest` build with
  it. The collection traces the examples' process tests (with server kills) and a probe of races
  (Write/Close, Write/CloseWrite, a reset or a kill mid-`Write`, deadlines, empty writes, dials with
  no listener, tiny `DialTimeout`s).
- **Search** (`examples/net/conform/check/`, Python): a transcription of `Impl.lean`'s relations and a
  linearizability search (Wing & Gong with Lowe's memo; lazy cuts and prefixes, with the completeness
  argument in its README; crashes between a node's processes; a `Write` as its loop's steps), which
  outputs a witness. 2026-11-15: 21 of 22 collected runs admitted (the semantics tests' own run out of
  scope: port 0, zero values, `SyscallConn`), none rejected; 786 of 786 mutants rejected.
- **Certification** (`GooseLang/Ext/Tcp/Decide.lean`, `Replay.lean`, `lake exe nettcp-replay`):
  deciders proved equivalent to the relations; the replay checks the plan, every witness step as a
  model step (`checkSteps_sound`: an accepted sequence is an execution of `TcpStep`/crash from the
  initial state), and every traced call linearized in its interval with its observed results (not
  proved; by reading). All 21 admitted runs and the 14 samples CERTIFIED; 19 tampered witnesses
  rejected. Trusted: the tracer, the records' copy, the error-facts-to-kinds rules (in Lean), a node's
  processes as its incarnations.

## Open

- `Conn.Read`'s count: `ReadOut` gives only `sint.nat k = min …` (so `k`'s sign is free when the
  minimum is 0), because Perennial's `wp_slice_copy` gives only that; a stronger copy spec in
  Perennial would pin it (`notes/perennial-changes.md`).
- `errors.Is(err, net.ErrClosed)` has no proof: Perennial's `errors.Is` spec admits only pointer
  targets, and `ErrClosed` is the value `errNetClosing{}`; `errors.Is` on a `sys` error is stuck (the
  `os` translation has no body for `(*SyscallError).Unwrap`) (`notes/perennial-changes.md`).
- Shared lemmas still local: deriving a direction's `inv` from an example's ghost-map invariant
  (`inv_alter`); a server writer bounded by another direction's bytes (`server_write_acc` in echo,
  framed, halfclose, acceptloop, preface: one `wire` form could replace them); `eof_init`.
- Precision (sound, more than Go): after a cut, a `Write` may succeed after its own `CloseWrite`; two
  concurrent `Write`s' steps may interleave (Go's write lock serializes them); across a crash, a
  restarted node may accept a connection dialed to its previous incarnation (the review's N1).
- acceptloop's `wp_Add_many` copies `examples/mvccrand/RunChecksJoin.lean`'s (the part cannot import
  the main tree's): a second copy of the `Add(n)` join spec Perennial lacks
  (`notes/perennial-changes.md`, "The `WaitGroup` join idiom's `Add(n)`").

## Address helpers (v3.3, phase 2.5 of the dsverify port)

go-vsr (`pkg/ipv4port`) calls `net.SplitHostPort`, `net.ParseIP`, `net.IP`'s `To4`, `String`,
`Equal`: pure Go, to be translated rather than modeled. What goose and Perennial allow (Go 1.27.3):

- `IP.To4` (and `isZeros`): length checks and slicing only. **Translated** (`Net/Code/net.toml`).
- `SplitHostPort`: `internal/bytealg.IndexByteString`, an assembly function on amd64
  (`indexbyte_native.go`, no Go body), which goose cannot translate: a trusted model of it would be
  needed (a loop; small, but a new trusted definition). Names only.
- `IP.Equal`: `bytealg.Equal` (Go: `string(a) == string(b)`) and the package variable
  `v4InV6Prefix`. `internal/bytealg` is owned by the etcd project's translation (`etc/goose.py`, the
  main tree's `Code/internal/bytealg.toml`, which translates only `LastIndexByteString`); adding
  `Equal` there regenerates that project and rebuilds every importer of `Code.internal.bytealg`, and
  the part `Net/` does not import it yet. Names only, for now.
- `ParseIP`, `IP.String`: `net/netip` (`ParseAddr`, `AddrFrom4(...).String()`), whose `Addr` holds a
  `unique.Handle[addrDetail]` made by `unique.Make` in netip's package initializer (`z4`, `z6noz`):
  `unique` is runtime internals (weak pointers, a concurrent map), out of goose's reach. Names only;
  a kit using them needs a trusted model of the few functions (or of netip's parse/format), decided
  with phase 4.
