## Verdict

**The PR keeps watch delivery correct.** The proved guarantee is unchanged:
- every event of a watcher's key is delivered once and in order;
- a watcher becomes synced only once it has every event up to the current revision;
- a victim's pending batch completes its delivery.

The PR's `syncWatchers` is proved against its existing spec with no `sorry`. The proof rests on one
new trust-boundary postulate, `sort.Slice`. The proof also shows the PR's bound: each cohort's
scan covers at most `watchBatchMaxRevs + 1` revisions.

**But the PR slows down watchers on quiet keys, and can starve them.** The new scan window is 1001
*revisions*, whether or not they hold events for the watcher. So a watcher on a rarely written key
advances at most about 10,000 revisions per second (one window per 100 ms pass). On `main` it
catches up in one pass. Under a sustained write rate above that, it never syncs. The Lean specs are
safety-only and cannot see this; Go tests show it (finding 1).

**Not covered by the proof:** compaction (the proof's model has no compaction), watchers moved to
`unsynced` by `Restore`, and every liveness property. The reviewer checked compaction and
`Restore` by Go tests: both are handled correctly.

**Compared with the other two PRs for #22264:** this is the only one that delivers every event.
- #22594 limits the scan by entry count and loses events (`pr-verify/etcd-22594`).
- #22265 bounds the scan window, but still moves every chosen watcher to `synced` at
  `curRev + 1`. It loses events: the randomized test fails on it.
- #22304's `reachedCurRev` is what makes the bounded window safe. It is the fix the #22594
  report's draft comment proposed, extended with cohorts.

### The specs

- **`syncWatchers`** (`Spec/go_etcd_io/etcd/server/v3/storage/mvcc/watchableStore__syncWatchers.lean`):
  unchanged. Under `s.mu` it keeps the watchable store's lock invariant (`watchStoreBody`,
  `InvWatchStore.lean`); only the docstring now describes the cohorts.
  - The proof (`Proof/.../mvcc/watchableStore__syncWatchers.lean`) takes the locks as before, calls
    `choose`, then `cohortsByRevision` on the chosen watchers, and rebuilds the lock's state.
  - It then loops over the cohorts. Each pass reads the cohort and computes the window's end `m`,
    which is `curRev + 1` or `c.minRev + watchBatchMaxRevs + 1`.
  - It then calls `syncWatcherCohort` on the current lock state. Its loop invariant is that the
    members of the cohorts not yet processed are unchanged: the cohorts are disjoint, and
    `syncWatcherCohort` only touches its own cohort.
- **`syncWatcherCohort`** (`Spec/.../mvcc/watchableStore__syncWatcherCohort.lean`, proof
  `Proof/.../mvcc/watchableStore__syncWatcherCohort.lean`):
  - It is called with the lock's state `σ` and the committed log `C`. The cohort is unsynced
    watchers with `minRev ≤ st.minRev < maxScanRev ≤ len(C)`, and the scan is bounded:
    `maxScanRev ≤ minRev + 1001`.
  - It scans `[minRev, maxScanRev)` and keeps the lock invariant. It changes `σ` only at the
    cohort's members, and the unsynced group only shrinks.
  - The proof is the old `syncWatchers` loop. The new cases where the scan did not reach `curRev`
    (no events; events with no `moreRev`; a victim with no `moreRev`) leave the watcher unsynced at
    `maxScanRev`, or a victim whose batch ends below `maxScanRev`. The existing invariant already
    allows these states (`popFacts`, `victimBatchOk`), so no new invariant was needed.
  - The bound `maxScanRev ≤ minRev + 1001` is the PR's purpose. This function's proof does not use
    it; `syncWatchers`' proof establishes it at each call.
- **`cohortsByRevision`** (`Spec/.../mvcc/cohortsByRevision.lean`; `CohortsOk` in
  `Proof/.../mvcc/Cohorts.lean`; proof `Proof/.../mvcc/cohortsByRevision.lean`): it takes the
  members `W` (with states `ST`, their `minRev`s ≥ 0) and returns fresh groups that satisfy
  `CohortsOk`:
  - the cohorts are disjoint subsets of `W`, and every member at or above `compactionRev` is in
    one;
  - each cohort is non-empty, and its start is one of its members' `minRev`;
  - every member is in `[start, start + 1000]`;
  - the cohorts are in increasing order, more than 1000 apart.

  **The window property depends on the sort.** If the members were not sorted, a later member with
  a smaller `minRev` would join an earlier cohort, and that cohort's scan would start above the
  member's `minRev`, losing events. The proof uses the sortedness that `sort.Slice` gives.

**Why the `sort.Slice` postulate is believed** (`Spec/sort/Slice.lean`, `Proof/sort/Slice.lean`):
- **The statement.** The slice (as an `any`) holds `xs`, and the caller's `less(i, j)` returns
  `key a < key b` for the elements of the slice's current contents, a permutation of `xs`. Then
  `sort.Slice` leaves a sorted permutation, and the caller's resource is kept.
- **Go's implementation fits it.** It sorts in place through `reflectlite.Swapper`, running
  `pdqsort_func` over `[0, len)`. So at every call of `less` the slice holds a permutation, and the
  indices are in range. A key order is a strict weak order.
- **The size bound.** At most `2^62` elements, as for this repository's `sort.Sort` and Perennial's
  `slices.SortFunc`.
- **The sorting is already proved elsewhere.** Perennial proves the same pdqsort for `sort.Sort`
  and `slices.SortFunc`. Only the reflection-based swapper keeps `sort.Slice` from being
  translated.
- **Checked by the reviewer:** this statement against `src/sort/slice.go`, and Goose's `any`
  conversion.

## Findings

1. **A watcher on a quiet key catches up slowly, and can starve: a liveness regression of the
   PR.** Not a safety bug, so there is no `issues/` entry (the pinned code is not affected).
   - **The cause.** On `main`, `watchBatchMaxRevs` caps the revisions *with events for the
     watcher* (`eventBatch.add`). So a watcher on a rarely written key is caught up from any depth
     in one pass. The PR caps the scanned window instead, and syncs a member only once its
     cohort's scan reaches `curRev`.
   - **The rate.** Such a watcher gains at most 1001 revisions per pass. Its unsynced count does
     not drop, so `syncWatchersLoop` waits 100 ms between passes: about 10k revisions per second.
     While unsynced it gets no new events and no progress notifications (`progressIfSync` needs
     `synced`).
   - **The tests**, in `notes/pr-verify/etcd-22304/`:
     - `watchable_store_sparse_catchup_test.go`: 1 event in 20,001 revisions takes 20 passes on the
       head and 1 on the base.
     - The reviewer's `TestReviewSparseWatcherPasses`: 5 passes against 1.
     - The reviewer's `TestReviewSparseWatcherStarves`: with 1500 revisions written between
       passes, the watcher is still unsynced after 50 passes on the head, and synced in pass 1 on
       the base.
   - **The same effect in #22594's counterexample test** (`scanlimit-22304.log`). It fails on this
     PR only through its 3 s timeout: with `watchBatchMaxRevs = 1` the window is 2 revisions, so
     catching up takes 55 s. With a longer timeout it passes, losing nothing.
   - **The PR description is off for these watchers.** It says that in subsequent passes watchers
     "will receive the full thousand". That holds only for watchers with an event at every
     revision.
   - **A possible remedy, not verified.** Sync a not-reached member with no events in the window
     when no event of its key exists up to `curRev`. Or let a member's window grow past 1001
     revisions while it has collected fewer than `watchBatchMaxRevs` revisions of events, which is
     the base's per-watcher cap. Either would need a cheaper per-key check than the full scan the
     PR removes.
2. **The per-pass scan is bounded per cohort, not per pass.**
   - Windows of different cohorts do not overlap, so one pass never scans more than `main`'s single
     scan did. It is at most 512 cohorts × 1001 revisions.
   - **Example:** 512 watchers whose `minRev`s are each more than 1000 apart still give a pass of
     about 512K revisions under `s.mu`.
   - This is weaker than the title suggests, but it is not a correctness problem.
3. **Confirmed correct, by the reviewer's Go tests** (outside the Lean model):
   - compaction: the compacted response is still sent by `chooseAll`, and
     `cohortsByRevision` skipping compacted watchers is harmless;
   - a future-revision watcher after `Restore` (minRev near `MaxInt64`) is synced in the first
     pass, with no overflow;
   - a victim from a cut window is resent with `Revision` = window end - 1, by `moveVictims`'
     `w.minRev - 1`;
   - the `Revision: curRev` header on partial batches is as on `main` for `moreRev` batches.
4. **A randomized delivery test** (`watchable_store_cohort_random_test.go`) passes on the head and
   the base, and fails on #22265. It uses random watchers, small channels (victims), and
   `watchBatchMaxRevs` from 1 to 6.

The PR fixes nothing recorded in `issues/`. No bug in the pinned code was found.

Reviewer's open points: the stale `syncWatchers` spec docstring, now fixed. And the fact that
`syncWatcherCohort`'s postcondition states the invariant and frame, not which population each
member goes to; that is intended, since the invariant is what callers rely on.

## Draft review comment

> Thanks, this keeps the delivery guarantee that a bounded window needs: a watcher is only moved
> to `synced` when the scan reached `curRev` (`reachedCurRev`), and otherwise stays unsynced at
> the window's end. Unlike the variants that keep `w.minRev = curRev+1`, no events are skipped.
> A randomized test with small `watchBatchMaxRevs`, several watchers per cohort and full channels
> (victims) delivers every event exactly once.
>
> One behavior change worth calling out: a watcher on a rarely written key now catches up at most
> `watchBatchMaxRevs+1` revisions per pass, about 10k revisions/s with the 100ms resync period.
> On `main` it catches up in a single pass, since `watchBatchMaxRevs` only counts revisions that
> have events for that watcher. For example, one event among 20,001 revisions takes 20 passes
> instead of 1. If more than about 1000 revisions are written per pass (about 10k writes/s), such a
> watcher never becomes synced, and gets no progress notifications meanwhile. Maybe sync a member early when its
> key has no events left up to `curRev`, or extend a member's window until it has collected
> `watchBatchMaxRevs` revisions of events?
>
> Minor: the bound is per cohort, so a pass with many far-apart cohorts (up to
> `maxWatchersPerSync`) can still scan about 512×1001 revisions.
